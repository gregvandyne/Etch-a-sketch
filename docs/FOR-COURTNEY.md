# Your website guide

Hi Courtney — this is the owner's manual for your new website. No code, no
jargon; everything here happens in your web browser.

## The one-minute version

Your website has a private dashboard at:

**courtneystockton.com/studio**

You sign in there (with your Sanity account — Google sign-in works), and
everything you publish appears on the website automatically within a few
seconds. You never need to touch code, call a developer, or "deploy" anything.

The dashboard is organized the way you work:

- **Portfolio** — your wedding, engagement and family galleries
- **Blog** — journal posts and their categories
- **Venues** — the wineries and estates you photograph at
- **Kind words** — client testimonials
- **Website pages** — the words and photos on the Homepage, About, Experience, Investment and Contact pages
- **Site settings** — your email, social links, service areas

## Uploading photographs

Photographs are uploaded right where you use them — inside a gallery, a post,
or a page. A few tips:

- **Upload your full-quality exports.** The website automatically creates
  every size it needs and serves fast, optimized versions to visitors. You
  never need to resize anything.
- **Bulk upload:** in any Photographs field, drag a whole batch of files from
  your computer straight into the grid. A curated wedding of 60–100 images
  uploads in one go.
- **Reorder by dragging.** The order in the dashboard is exactly the order on
  the website.
- **Focal point:** click a photo, then drag the small circle to the most
  important part of the frame. Wherever the website crops (covers,
  thumbnails), it keeps that spot in view.
- **Photo description (alt text):** a short sentence like "Bride and groom
  laughing during golden hour at Viansa." It helps Google find your work and
  helps visitors who use screen readers. Worth doing for at least the cover
  photo of each gallery.

## Creating a wedding gallery (the full workflow)

1. Open **Portfolio → Weddings** and click the compose (pencil) button.
2. Give it a **title** — "Emily & James — Viansa Sonoma Wedding" reads well.
3. Click **Generate** under Web address.
4. Fill in **Location** and pick the **Venue** (or create a new venue right
   from that field — takes ten seconds).
5. Set the **date** (used for ordering; visitors only ever see the month/year).
6. Switch to the **Photographs** tab: drag in the whole batch, reorder by
   dragging, then choose the **cover photograph**.
7. Switch to the **Story** tab and write a short introduction (one to three
   sentences is plenty).
8. Press **Publish**.

That's it. The gallery immediately appears in the Weddings portfolio, on its
venue's page, in the sitemap, and (if you turn on "Feature on the homepage")
on the homepage. Engagements and families work exactly the same way from
their own sections.

## Writing a journal post

1. **Blog → Posts** → compose.
2. Title, Generate the address, pick a **featured photograph**, write a
   **short summary** (it shows in the blog overview and in Google).
3. Write the story. The + button between paragraphs adds photographs,
   side-by-side pairs, galleries, quotes and buttons — the design always
   stays on-brand automatically.
4. On the **Connections** tab, link the related **gallery** and **venue** —
   the post then shows a "view the full gallery" invitation, and the venue
   page lists the post. No copying anything twice.
5. **Publish** (or leave it unpublished as a draft until you're ready).

**Moving over an old post from the previous site:** every post from your
old website is already waiting in **Blog → Posts** as a draft, with its
title, original web address and category filled in. Open one, add the
photographs and the story, set the publish date, and press Publish. The old
link and its Google ranking carry straight over. (Until a post is
published, its old address quietly sends visitors to the matching portfolio
section, so nothing ever hits a dead end.)

## Venues

Create a venue once (**Venues** → compose): name, location, a photo, a few
sentences about photographing there. Every gallery you link to it appears on
its page automatically — "Weddings photographed at Viansa" builds itself.

## Kind words

**Kind words** → compose: the client's words exactly as written, their names,
and the session type. Toggle **Show on the homepage** for your favorites, and
use the order number to arrange them. Only ever add real reviews.

## Editing page content

**Website pages** holds the words on the site's main pages — the homepage
headline, your About story, the Experience steps, Investment offerings and
the Contact welcome note. Each field is labeled in plain language and shows
where it appears. Edit, Publish, done.

Pricing note: the Investment page shows a price only if you type one into an
offering's "Starting investment" field. Leave it empty and no price appears.

## Drafts and previewing

Nothing is public until you press **Publish**. You can save half-finished
work freely, and it stays a draft. To see exactly how a draft will look,
open the **Preview** tab at the top of the dashboard: it shows the real
website with your unpublished changes visible, side by side with the
editor. Publishing takes effect on the live site within a few seconds. To
unpublish something, open it and choose Unpublish from the menu next to the
Publish button; the page comes down right away.

## Search settings (SEO)

Every gallery, post, venue and page has a **Search & sharing** tab. You can
ignore it completely — good titles and descriptions are generated
automatically. If you want to fine-tune how something looks in Google or when
texted/shared, that's where you do it: a custom headline, a two-sentence
description, and the image used when the link is shared.

## Inquiries

Inquiry-form submissions arrive in your email inbox with everything the
couple filled in — including which gallery or page they were looking at
before they inquired (useful for knowing what's working). Replying to the
email replies directly to them.

## If something looks wrong

Check the document in the dashboard first — a missing photo or empty field
there means a missing photo on the site. Every change is versioned, so
nothing is ever truly lost: open the document menu → Review changes to see
history and restore any earlier version.
